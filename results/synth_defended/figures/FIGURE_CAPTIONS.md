## fig1_latency_by_config.png

*Goes in section 4.2 / 4.6.*

Distribution of per-trial median send latency for each firewall configuration, with the noise-injection defence inactive and active. Separation between configurations is the side channel; the upward shift under the defence is the latency cost paid by legitimate traffic. Boxes span the interquartile range, whiskers 1.5 IQR; outliers omitted for legibility.

## fig4_confusion_randomforest.png

*Goes in section 4.3 / 4.4.*

Row-normalised confusion matrices for the randomforest attacker. Rows sum to one, so the diagonal is per-class recall and off-diagonal mass shows which configurations the attacker cannot separate. Whether errors concentrate along verdict or along rule depth indicates which of the two secrets the channel actually carries.

## fig5_feature_importance.png

*Goes in section 4.2.*

Random forest feature importances, ordered by the undefended condition. Which statistic dominates says what the channel is: central-tendency features indicate a deterministic cost difference, while spread or tail features indicate the leak is carried by variability. Impurity-based importance is biased toward high-cardinality features and is used here descriptively, not as inference.
