## fig3_sweep_curve.png

*Goes in section 4.5.*

Attacker balanced accuracy as a function of observation budget. If the defended curve recovers toward the undefended one, the defence raises the cost of the attack rather than preventing it; the horizontal separation at the target accuracy is the trace-multiplication factor. A defended curve that stays flat at chance would instead indicate the leak had been removed within the budget tested. On this dataset the four-class curve saturates - see the verdict-axis figure for the measurement that resolves.

## fig3b_sweep_curve_verdict.png

*Goes in section 4.5.*

Attacker balanced accuracy against observation budget on the verdict axis (accept vs drop within the deep configurations, chance 0.5). This axis rather than the four-class problem, because chain depth is separable from roughly a single packet: the four-class curve opens above its own half-way target, so its trace-multiplication factor degenerates to 1.0 and measures nothing. Separation between the curves is the cost the defence imposes; convergence at the largest budgets is the attacker recovering, which is the outcome Chari et al. (1999) predict for noise countermeasures. Mean separation across the swept budgets is +0.061 balanced accuracy.

## fig5_feature_importance.png

*Goes in section 4.2.*

Random forest feature importances, ordered by the undefended condition. Which statistic dominates says what the channel is: central-tendency features indicate a deterministic cost difference, while spread or tail features indicate the leak is carried by variability. Impurity-based importance is biased toward high-cardinality features and is used here descriptively, not as inference.
